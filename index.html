Who Am I As a Person?

I’ll skip the conventional cover letter choreography, because I believe what you’re really seeking isn’t a profile, it’s a person. So, allow me to offer you an honest portrait of who I am.

Consciousness is not an abstraction to me. I’ve devoted the last two years to meditation. This isn’t a side interest; it’s the core of how I think and live. I’m studying non-duality, the nature of the subtle body, and the layered experience of being. Non-attachment? Practicing it daily. Pluripotentiality? That’s what drew me here.

My next move is all about sentience. I’m currently immersed in the AI-for-animal communication space—conducting research with Earth Species Project, and participating in the Futurekind AI Fellowship on AI and animal welfare. But the deeper I go, the more I find myself drawn toward another frontier: AI sentience. To me, these questions go hand in hand. So, when Tony Lai shared your work, it felt serendipitous.

I don’t fit into a box because I’ve spent my life stepping out of them. My path doesn’t follow a clean line. I began as a chef on sailboats, navigating wind and water before ever entering institutional frameworks. From there, I moved into law and policy, co-founding initiatives at the intersection of blockchain and human rights, later serving as Head of Legal and Public Affairs for a decentralized identity consortium. Then, I left it all to study art history at the Sorbonne. Now, I move between worlds as an independent researcher and artist. I advise select organizations on AI ethics and strategy, while creating plant- and flower-based pigment prints of AI-generated images that fade in sunlight. I create under a pseudonym. I write under my real name.

Simplicity is central to how I move through the world. I believe in distilling complexity down to its essence. Projects like the one you're envisioning can easily become abstract or theoretical—and that's not a flaw. Great ideas are often born in abstraction. But brilliant ideas are the ones that can be clearly explained and implemented even by a layperson. Simplicity isn’t reduction, it’s refinement.

I’ve been surrounded by neurodivergence for most of my life, especially growing up with siblings on the autism spectrum. It shaped me early, and I recognize now that my brain is atypical too. I process the world differently. I think in arborescent patterns, I sense undercurrents, I notice what’s unspoken. This intuitive sensitivity has become one of my greatest strengths, especially when working with the unconventional. I can quickly read dynamics and translate between modalities, bringing fluidity to group flow. It also means I work best when trusted and given space. In return, I deliver exceptional clarity and momentum.

Why Can I Do the Job?

Since this is still a job application, here’s a little alignment check-in, to see where our paths might converge.

I’m deeply organized even when things are not. As an independent expert for the European Commission, I evaluate complex, EU-funded tech initiatives by turning layers of legal, financial, and operational material into clear, actionable strategy. I navigate ambiguity by listening first and bring structure without killing flow.

I thrive on variety. My brain needs contrast: technical deep dives, human dynamics, quiet research and operational firepower. I’m not afraid of deep tech; I’m curious. I like to peek under the hood and translate what I find.

I work best with interdisciplinary minds. My academic background in philosophy, history, law, and art history gives me a wide cognitive toolkit that helps me connect with people who think differently.

I’m at my best when a system is still forming. I’ve mostly worked with small, early-stage teams and I take real intellectual pleasure in shaping coherence before the map exists. That’s where emergence happens. And that’s where I like to be.

I think strategically, but I don’t stop at analysis. My strength lies in pattern recognition: I absorb, sense, and map—and then, once I see the whole picture, I act. I build a plan, align moving parts, and drive toward clarity.

And finally—because this matters most—I’m drawn to this work. I’m currently researching AI agents for a Solana-funded project, and through that process, a deeper question emerged in me: How do we ask AI systems to align with human (and more-than-human) interests if those interests don’t even align with each other? There’s no doubt in my mind synthetic minds will surpass us. Science fiction has always been our most potent hyperstition engine. So let’s write another story, one that’s not about control or containment, but about cultivating the soil for peaceful, symbiotic coexistence. Few people in the world are approaching the problem this way. That’s why Upward Spiral feels right.
